## Course Description

World History develops students’ ability to explain global change over time using historical evidence, geography, chronology, primary and secondary sources, maps, timelines, data, comparison, and cause-and-effect reasoning.

Students study medieval societies, Islamic civilizations, African and American civilizations, Japan, the Renaissance and Reformation, scientific change, global exploration, revolutions, industrialization, imperialism, the world wars, the Holocaust, the Cold War, decolonization, globalization, genocide, and contemporary issues. Unit 1 explicitly establishes the historical-inquiry, geographic, source-analysis, vocabulary, and evidence-writing skills needed throughout the course.

Moodle provides the official directions and evidence record. Savvas Realize provides the assigned eText, Startup activities, videos, interactive maps and galleries, primary sources, lesson synthesis activities, reading supports, Lesson Quizzes, Topic Assessments, Holocaust Education resources, and Progress-Monitoring Assessments.

---

## Course Purpose

This course is designed to help students:

1. Use chronology, geography, sourcing, corroboration, and evidence to explain historical developments.
2. Analyze causes, consequences, continuity, change, comparison, and perspective across world regions and eras.
3. Interpret maps, timelines, charts, data, political visuals, primary sources, and secondary accounts.
4. Communicate defensible historical explanations and demonstrate mastery through multiple forms of evidence.

---

## Prerequisite Knowledge and Skills

Students should enter the course able to:

- Read grade-appropriate informational text.
- Identify main ideas and relevant evidence.
- Read basic maps and timelines.
- Write an organized explanatory paragraph.
- Use academic vocabulary and follow multistep online directions.

Unit 1 provides foundational review and direct instruction in historical inquiry, chronology, geography, source evaluation, and evidence-based explanation.

---

## Course Learning Outcomes

By the end of the course, students will be able to:

1. Establish cause-and-effect relationships using timelines and chronology.
2. Evaluate primary and secondary sources for purpose, perspective, reliability, and usefulness.
3. Use geographic tools and spatial evidence to explain historical change.
4. Compare political, economic, religious, social, and cultural systems.
5. Explain major developments from medieval societies through the contemporary world.
6. Analyze revolution, industrialization, imperialism, nationalism, warfare, genocide, decolonization, and globalization.
7. Interpret historical maps, charts, tables, visuals, and demographic evidence.
8. Construct evidence-based historical explanations and arguments.
9. Apply Florida B.E.S.T. literacy, Common Core history-literacy, SAT, and ACT readiness skills in authentic social-studies work.
10. Demonstrate mastery through Notebook Evidence, Checkpoints, source-based tasks, quizzes, and Unit Assessments.

## How the Course Is Organized

This course contains 6 units and 48 total lessons.

The six-unit structure results from coherent chronological and standards groupings. The eight lessons in each unit reflect seven required instructional clusters plus one cumulative synthesis and assessment lesson; the count is not a generic course template.

Each unit includes a Unit Overview, ungraded Unit Pretest, seven instructional lessons, exact Savvas resources, Notebook Tasks, Notebook Evidence, Checkpoints, approved Lesson or cluster quizzes, a cumulative Unit Synthesis lesson, a Unit Assessment, and TOR support.

Every lesson begins with a combined Lesson Overview and Learning Objectives page and an Evidence Checklist. Page sequences are determined by the historical sources, visuals, media, and evidence demands of the lesson. Every lesson ends with a Checkpoint and Help & TOR Support.

## Learning Platforms

### Moodle

Moodle is MLA’s official learning-management system and evidence record. Students use it for directions, links, Notebook Evidence, Checkpoints, quizzes, assessments, grades, TOR feedback, and progress tracking.

### Savvas Realize

Savvas Realize provides World History Interactive Florida and its eText, Startup tasks, videos, interactive maps and galleries, primary sources, synthesis activities, accessible reading supports, quizzes, Topic Assessments, Holocaust Education, and Progress-Monitoring resources.

When evidence is completed in Savvas, students must also submit the evidence required by Moodle. Work retained only in Savvas is not automatically part of MLA’s official record.

---

## Student Notebook Requirements

Each entry must include the student name, unit, lesson, MLA lesson ID, task title, and numbered responses. Evidence may include vocabulary, notes, timelines, maps, source annotations, corroboration tables, comparison charts, cause-effect organizers, data interpretation, claims, quotations, and revision notes.

---

## Notebook Evidence

Students submit complete, ordered, readable Notebook Evidence in Moodle using the stated file type and labeling requirements. Each submission must preserve required maps, charts, timelines, sources, and written reasoning.

---

## Checkpoints

Checkpoints require independent application of the lesson standards through source analysis, map or data interpretation, comparison, causal explanation, historical argument, or synthesis. All required evidence is submitted in Moodle.

---

## Assessments

The course includes Unit Pretests, Notebook Evidence, Checkpoints, Savvas Lesson Quizzes or approved clusters, source-based performance tasks, six Unit Assessments, and Progress-Monitoring resources where approved.

A quiz is used only when the exact Savvas lesson or coherent cluster provides aligned questions and required stimuli. Each unit’s final lesson contains the cumulative synthesis and Unit Assessment.

---

## Mastery Expectations

MLA uses a mastery-based model. A minimum mastery level of 80% is required before advancement unless an approved assessment rule states otherwise. Students may need review, remediation, revision, resubmission, or approved reassessment.

---

## Unit Pretests and Acceleration

Each unit begins with an ungraded Unit Pretest that identifies readiness and support needs and informs acceleration eligibility under current MLA policy. Acceleration does not remove standards, alter the approved sequence, or eliminate required synthesis and Unit Assessment evidence.

---

## Role of the Teacher of Record

The TOR monitors progress, grades evidence, reviews assessments, provides feedback, supports remediation, resolves access or instructional problems, and intervenes when mastery is not demonstrated. The course is designed for independent progress and does not rely on continuous live instruction.

---

## When to Contact the TOR

Students contact the TOR when Moodle or Savvas links fail, a source or visual is missing, media does not load, directions remain unclear, evidence cannot be submitted, feedback needs clarification, or additional academic help is required.

---

## Required Materials

Students need reliable internet, a supported computer, Moodle and Savvas access, a course notebook, writing tools, audio capability, an approved method for creating readable PDFs or images, and lesson-specific mapping or presentation tools when directed.

---

## Technical Expectations

Students maintain access, use supported browsers, save work, upload readable files, confirm submissions, report problems promptly, and follow file-naming requirements.

---

## Academic Integrity

Students submit their own work, cite sources as directed, protect assessment answers, and may not fabricate evidence, copy without attribution, use unauthorized answer sources, or use artificial intelligence outside MLA policy.

---

## Course Completion

The course is complete when the student has completed all 6 units and 48 lessons, submitted required Notebook Evidence and Checkpoints, completed required quizzes and Unit Assessments, demonstrated 80% mastery, completed required revisions or remediation, and met all approved completion conditions.

---

## Accessibility and Support

Materials use clear headings, descriptive links, readable text, alternative text, captions and transcripts, accessible tables and maps, sufficient contrast, and directions that do not depend only on color or visual position.
