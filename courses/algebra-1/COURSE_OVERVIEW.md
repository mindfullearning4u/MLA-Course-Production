## Course Description

Algebra 1 develops students’ ability to represent, analyze, and solve mathematical and real-world problems using equations, inequalities, functions, systems, exponents, radicals, polynomials, quadratics, and data. Students build conceptual understanding, procedural fluency, modeling skills, reasoning, and precise mathematical communication.

Students follow lesson directions and submit official evidence in Moodle while using Savvas Realize for the primary eText, examples, videos, interactive resources, practice, modeling activities, and approved assessments. Lessons combine Notebook Tasks, worked examples, independent application, Checkpoints, quizzes when assigned, and cumulative Unit Assessments.

The course prepares students for later high-school mathematics, college and career quantitative reasoning, Florida assessment expectations, and related SAT and ACT skill domains. Florida B.E.S.T. Algebra 1 benchmarks remain the primary requirements; Common Core, SAT, and ACT references provide secondary readiness alignment.

---

## Course Purpose

This course is designed to help students:

1. Develop a durable understanding of algebraic structures, representations, and relationships.
2. Solve and model real-world and mathematical problems with equations, inequalities, systems, and functions.
3. Analyze linear, exponential, quadratic, absolute-value, radical, cubic, and data-based models.
4. Demonstrate mastery through organized mathematical reasoning, Notebook Evidence, Checkpoints, and assessments.

---

## Prerequisite Knowledge and Skills

Students should enter the course able to:

- Perform basic operations with whole numbers, integers, fractions, decimals, and percents.
- Use ratios, rates, and proportional reasoning.
- Evaluate and simplify basic numerical and algebraic expressions.
- Read coordinate graphs, tables, and common data displays.
- Communicate mathematical work using appropriate notation and complete reasoning.

Unit 1 provides foundational review and instruction in the prerequisite skills required for success in this course.

---

## Course Learning Outcomes

By the end of the course, students will be able to:

1. Interpret and rewrite expressions using exponent, radical, and polynomial properties.
2. Solve and model with linear equations, inequalities, absolute-value relationships, and systems.
3. Represent and compare linear, exponential, quadratic, square-root, cubic, and cube-root functions.
4. Analyze domain, range, rate of change, transformations, intercepts, extrema, and other key features.
5. Solve quadratic equations using graphs, factoring, square roots, completing the square, and the quadratic formula.
6. Represent and analyze numerical, categorical, and bivariate data.
7. Interpret association, lines of fit, residuals, correlation, causation, and two-way frequency tables.
8. Apply algebra to financial-literacy and other authentic contexts.
9. Explain reasoning and synthesize multiple concepts in cumulative unit tasks.

## How the Course Is Organized

This course contains 8 units and 64 total lessons.

Students complete one course at a time through MLA’s mastery-based model.

Each unit includes:

- A Unit Overview
- A Unit Pretest
- Required lessons
- Savvas instructional resources
- Notebook Tasks
- Notebook Evidence submissions
- Checkpoints
- Practice or formative assessments
- A cumulative Unit Synthesis lesson
- A Unit Assessment
- TOR support when needed

Lesson page sequences vary according to the instructional needs of the content.

Every lesson begins with:

- Lesson Overview
- Learning Objectives
- Evidence Checklist

Each lesson includes the pages and activities required to teach the approved content effectively. Every lesson ends with a Checkpoint and Help & TOR Support. A quiz is included only when the approved assessment map places a lesson-level or cluster quiz at that point.

## Unit Pretests and Acceleration

Each unit begins with an ungraded Unit Pretest.

The Unit Pretest identifies anticipated support needs and determines whether the student may qualify for the separately administered accelerated path for that unit. A score of 90% or higher establishes acceleration eligibility.

Eligibility does not automatically change the approved unit map, lesson sequence, standards requirements, or required Unit Assessment. Pathway decisions are administered separately under current MLA policy. Accelerated students may receive approved workload adjustments, such as a lighter Notebook Task, without removing required standards or cumulative mastery evidence.

## Role of the Teacher of Record

The Teacher of Record (TOR):

- Monitors student progress
- Grades required evidence
- Reviews assessments
- Provides feedback
- Supports remediation
- Helps resolve access or instructional issues
- Intervenes when a student is not demonstrating mastery

The TOR does not provide continuous live instruction.

The course directions are designed so students can proceed independently.
