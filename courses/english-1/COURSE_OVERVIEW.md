## Course Description

English 1 develops Grade 9 reading, writing, language, research, and communication skills through literary, informational, visual, audio, and multimedia texts. Students analyze literary elements, universal themes, narrator perspective, epic poetry, text structure, central ideas, rhetoric, figurative language, adaptations, and opposing arguments.

Students follow the official course sequence in Moodle and use Savvas Realize for assigned texts, accessible readings, videos, media, close-reading resources, vocabulary and language instruction, writing models, performance tasks, practice, and assessments. Moodle contains the authoritative directions and official evidence record.

The course prepares students for later high-school English, college and career communication, Florida B.E.S.T. Grade 9 ELA expectations, and related SAT Reading and Writing and ACT English and Reading domains.

---

## Course Purpose

This course is designed to help students:

1. Analyze complex literary, informational, and multimedia texts using relevant evidence.
2. Write effective narrative, argumentative, and expository texts.
3. Apply academic vocabulary, Standard English, research, and revision strategies.
4. Present and publish coherent ideas through oral, digital, and collaborative communication.

---

## Prerequisite Knowledge and Skills

Students should enter the course able to:

- Identify central ideas and themes in grade-appropriate texts.
- Paraphrase passages and select relevant textual evidence.
- Write organized paragraphs with a clear focus and support.
- Apply foundational conventions and academic vocabulary.
- Participate responsibly in independent digital learning.

Unit 1 provides foundational instruction in the prerequisite skills required for success in this course. It is already built and approved and is not part of the new production scope.

---

## Course Learning Outcomes

By the end of the course, students will be able to:

1. Explain how literary elements create meaning and trace universal themes.
2. Analyze narrator perspective, irony, satire, epic conventions, and adaptations.
3. Analyze structure, central-idea support, rhetoric, figurative language, and mood.
4. Compare opposing arguments and evaluate claims and evidence.
5. Write narratives, arguments, and expository texts for authentic purposes.
6. Improve writing through feedback and revision.
7. Conduct research using reliable sources and refine inquiry.
8. Apply Standard English and academic vocabulary.
9. Present, publish, and collaborate using oral and digital tools.

## How the Course Is Organized

This course contains 6 units and 56 total lessons.

Students complete one course at a time through MLA’s mastery-based model.

Each unit includes a Unit Overview, Unit Pretest, required lessons, Savvas resources, Notebook Tasks, Notebook Evidence, Checkpoints, formative assessment, a cumulative Unit Synthesis lesson, a Unit Assessment, and TOR support. Units 2–6 include 12 approved cluster quizzes.

Every lesson begins with:

- Lesson Overview
- Learning Objectives
- Evidence Checklist

Each lesson includes the pages and activities required to teach the approved content. Units 2–6 use the approved seven-page English lesson patterns and end each lesson with a Checkpoint and Help & TOR Support.

## Learning Platforms

### Moodle

Moodle is MLA’s official learning-management system and evidence record. Students use it to follow instructions, access resources, submit Notebook Evidence and Checkpoints, complete quizzes and assessments, submit writing and presentations, review feedback, and track progress.

### Savvas Realize

Savvas Realize provides the primary eText and approved instructional resources. Students use it for assigned texts, accessible text and audio, videos, media, close reading, vocabulary, writing models, practice, performance tasks, and assessments.

When Moodle requires evidence from Savvas, students must also submit that evidence in Moodle. Work retained only in Savvas is not automatically part of MLA’s official evidence record.

---

## Student Notebook Requirements

Each notebook entry must include the student name, unit, lesson, MLA lesson ID, task title, and numbered responses when applicable. Required evidence may include vocabulary, annotations, paraphrases, evidence logs, analysis charts, writing plans, drafts, revision notes, source evaluations, research logs, and presentation planning.

---

## Notebook Evidence

Students submit Notebook Evidence in Moodle as directed. They must complete every required item, place evidence in order, ensure readability, include unit and lesson identification, use the approved file type, and verify the upload before finalizing.

---

## Checkpoints

Checkpoints demonstrate application of lesson learning. They may require close reading, evidence-based analysis, comparison, a writing product, research evidence, presentation evidence, or a synthesis task. All required Checkpoint evidence is submitted in Moodle.

---

## Assessments

The course includes Unit Pretests, Notebook Evidence, Checkpoints, writing and research products, presentations, performance tasks, approved cluster quizzes, and Unit Assessments.

The Unit and Lesson Map identifies the approved structure. A quiz is not required after every lesson. Units 2–6 contain 12 cluster quizzes. Unit 1 retains its existing approved assessment structure. Every Unit Assessment is located in the final cumulative Unit Synthesis lesson.

---

## Mastery Expectations

MLA uses a mastery-based model. Students must meet the mastery threshold established by approved MLA policy and configured in Moodle. Students may be required to review content, revise evidence or writing, resubmit a Checkpoint, complete remediation, or attempt an approved reassessment. Progress is based on demonstrated mastery, not seat time alone.

---

## Unit Pretests and Acceleration

Each unit begins with a Unit Pretest that identifies prerequisite strengths and support needs and may determine eligibility for an approved accelerated path. Acceleration decisions follow current MLA policy and do not alter the approved standards, unit or lesson sequence, required synthesis products, or Unit Assessments. This overview does not insert an assumed threshold.

---

## Role of the Teacher of Record

The TOR monitors progress, grades required evidence, reviews assessments and performance products, provides feedback, supports remediation and revision, resolves access or instructional issues, and intervenes when mastery is not demonstrated. The TOR does not provide continuous live instruction.

---

## When to Contact the TOR

Students should contact the TOR when a Moodle or Savvas link fails, a text or accessible resource is missing, media does not load, instructions remain unclear, a submission cannot be uploaded, feedback needs clarification, or additional support is needed. Include the course, unit, lesson, page or activity, issue description, and screenshot when useful.

---

## Required Materials

Students need reliable internet, a computer or approved device, Moodle and Savvas access, a course notebook, writing materials, audio equipment for media, a recording method when oral evidence is required, and a way to create readable scans or PDFs.

---

## Technical Expectations

Students must maintain platform access, use supported browsers, save work, upload readable files, confirm submissions, report problems promptly, and follow file-naming requirements.

---

## Academic Integrity

Students must submit their own work and may not share answers, use unauthorized sources, use artificial intelligence outside MLA policy, copy without attribution, fabricate evidence, or misrepresent collaboration.

---

## Course Completion

The course is complete when the student has completed all 6 units and 56 lessons, submitted required Notebook Evidence and Checkpoints, completed required writing, research, presentation, quizzes, and Unit Assessments, demonstrated mastery, completed revisions or remediation, and met all approved completion conditions.

---

## Accessibility and Support

Course materials use clear headings, descriptive links, readable text, alternative text, captions, transcripts or accessible audio alternatives, accessible tables, sufficient contrast, and directions that do not rely only on color or position.
